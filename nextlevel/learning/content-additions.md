### I2. The lead magnet post structure (inbound)

The notes' structure, in four beats:

1. Name one problem the reader has this week.
2. Say what the free guide lets them do about it tonight.
3. Ask them to comment one word to get it.
4. Reply to each comment with the link to a short form: first name, email, optional mobile, and a consent box that is not pre-ticked.

The comment is a request for the guide. It is not consent to marketing. The form's consent box decides who gets follow-up.

Draft post:

> You were in a showing last night. Your phone rang. Who answered?
>
> I made a one-page test you can run on your own business line tonight. It takes 10 minutes and it costs nothing.
>
> Comment TEST and I will send it to you.

### I3. Lead magnet: The 8 PM Test (inbound)

A one-page self-audit. The reader calls their own business line from another phone at 8 PM and writes down: rings before an answer, what picked up, whether the greeting names them, whether a text came back, how long a callback took.

Last line of the page: "If you did not like what you heard, take the 6-question quiz. {{quiz link}}"

Why it fits: it is the missed-call audit, done by the agent, with no call from us. It asks for no claim from Next Level.

### I8. Hook structures (inbound)

Structures taken from the notes, each with a Next Level example. Hooks are openers for video, carousels and posts.

**The after-hours question.** Ask what happens at a specific bad moment.
- "A buyer fills in your website form at 10 PM on a Friday. What happens next?"
- "It is 8 PM. You are at dinner. Your business line rings. Then what?"
- "You are in a listing appointment for 90 minutes. Three people call. Who talks to them?"

**The trigger and the action.** Explain a system as one event and what follows.
- "A call is missed. Ten seconds later the caller gets a text with a booking link. That is the whole system."
- "A deal closes. A review request goes out. Thirty days later, a second one. You did nothing."

**The result without the pain.** One line with two halves.
- "Every call answered, without you stepping out of the showing."
- "Every client asked for a review, without you remembering."

**The thing most people get wrong first.**
- "Agents buy ads before they fix the phone. Fix the phone first."
- "Agents message their whole database before they sort it. Sort it first."

**The honest no.**
- "If you rarely miss a call, you do not need an AI receptionist. Here is how to check."

**Client zero.**
- "I do not sell anything I do not run on my own business. Here is my phone line. Call it."
- "This is the AI receptionist on my own line. I am going to call it now and you can listen."

### I9. Teaching posts (inbound)

Short educational pieces, from the foundations section of the notes.

1. **What an automation is, in one picture.** A trigger, then actions, with waits in between. Example: a booking, a confirmation, a reminder the day before, a reschedule message if nobody confirms.
2. **The five places an agent's business leaks.** Slow replies to web leads, calls after hours, few reviews, contacts scattered across a phone and a spreadsheet, ads nobody watches. Ends with the quiz.
3. **What to ignore when you buy software.** Most tools do twenty things. Pick the one job that fixes your biggest leak and ignore the rest for 30 days.
4. **Voicemail against a receptionist.** Ray plays his old voicemail greeting, then calls the AI receptionist live. No commentary until the end.
5. **Why I say it is an AI.** The receptionist tells every caller it is an AI assistant, and anyone can ask for a person. Ray explains why he set it that way.
