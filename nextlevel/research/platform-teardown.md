## d. How the business runs, as far as public pages show

**Acquisition.** One content engine feeds three offers. The personal site carries a public how-to hub on HighLevel that ranks for buyer and builder questions and carries an affiliate signup link. The free community is the top of the education side. The firm's own site sells the done-for-you service with role pages, industry pages, case studies and a blog. Every page ends on one action: book the free consult.

**Qualification.** The consult form asks for a revenue band, headcount, the tools that run the business, what the owner would hand over first and when they want to start. The stated promise is a recommendation with a fixed scope and price, and "if we are not the right fit, we say so".

**Onboarding.** For clients: map how calls or leads arrive, build and connect, test until real questions are handled, then go live. The client approves messages first. For licensees: an onboarding course, with support queries handled for them at the start. For affiliate signups: a setup call inside 5 days and a group onboarding call.

**Support.** Live chat for licensees, a community, weekly live calls. For clients the service is described as built and run by the firm, with conversations reviewed and tuned.

**Retention.** A monthly report of the numbers. Continuous tuning. Community. For licensees, leaving closes the account and its sub-accounts, which makes leaving costly.

**Upsell.** Start with the one role that matters most, then add roles: each role page links to "the rest of the team". On the education side: free program, then paid program, then partner certification. The licence is offered to people who came for the training.

## e. What we take, what we skip, what we must not do

### What we take

Ideas and structures only, each rebuilt in our own words on our own account. Ranked by value to Ray's first 10 clients.

1. **Run and report as a fixed routine.** A set day each month for the report, and a set day each week to read calls and tune the prompt. It is the retention tool that costs nothing to build. (Blueprint 7.)
2. **The urgent path with a summary to the owner's phone.** The owner gets the caller's name, number and reason in one text and calls back without asking the caller to repeat. (Blueprint 1.)
3. **A cap the client controls.** Not from their pages: from HighLevel's own usage-limit settings read on the same day. It turns promise 2 from a hope into a setting. (Blueprint 8.)
4. **Call mapping before the build.** Ask how calls arrive today, what callers ask, and what counts as a good booking, then write the question slots from the answers. (Onboarding step 2.)
5. **Test calls against real questions before go-live.** A written 10-call script with a pass mark. (Blueprint 1.)
6. **Message sign-off.** The client reads and approves every text and email before a workflow is switched on. (Onboarding step 8.)
7. **Missed-call text-back that does not repeat itself.** One text per caller per day, not one per missed call. (Blueprint 2.)
8. **Two better intake questions.** "What runs your business today" and "what would you hand over first". (Quiz.)
9. **A plain comparison table** built from our own facts: Next Level Answer, a human answering service, a self-serve app.
10. **Role-style naming of outcomes.** Their six roles each name one job. Our rungs already do this (Answer, Reviews, Nurture). Keep each rung to one job and one number.
11. **Short client training videos**, our own, for the three things a client does in the app.
12. **Buyer-question articles** for Ontario agents: what it costs, what the caller hears, what happens to the recording.

### What we must not do

1. **Copy any asset.** No snapshot, workflow, prompt, script, template, page copy, image, layout, case study or review quote of theirs is imported, pasted or paraphrased closely. The snapshot import link mentioned in Ray's course notes is not used as a base for anything sold.
2. **Reuse their numbers.** No client count, revenue figure, rating or case result from their pages appears in Next Level material.
3. **Imply affiliation.** Not "trained by", "certified by", "partner of", "the same system as" or "licensed from". Their names do not appear in Next Level marketing at all.
4. **Use their product names.** "AI employee" is also the name of a HighLevel product. Next Level uses its own names.
5. **Make income or results claims.** Nothing like "profitable after your first sale". Next Level states what the system does and shows real counts from Ray's own line.
6. **Go behind a login.** No trial, signup or purchase made to see member material. If Ray is a paying member of something, that material stays for his own study and is not copied into the build.
7. **Publish the saved pages.** `research/pages/` is private reference.
8. **Present HighLevel facts as Next Level's own.** Certifications, hosting and prices belong to HighLevel, change often and are quoted with their source.

## What this changes for Next Level, in five lines

1. The voice agent and its actions travel inside the snapshot. Only the phone number is set by hand per client.
2. A client-controlled cap is a real setting. The promise can be kept by the platform, once the blocked-call behaviour is tested.
3. Much of the snapshot's skeleton (custom values, fields, tags, pipeline, calendar, the voice agent itself) can be created through the API. Workflows can be only if HighLevel has enabled the workflow API for that sub-account.
4. "At cost" needs one honest footnote: with rebilling on, HighLevel adds 5% to carrier and registration fees.
5. Punjabi and Dari are not on any official list. Persian, Urdu, Arabic and "Chinese" are on the longer list and still need a test call before anyone is told yes.
