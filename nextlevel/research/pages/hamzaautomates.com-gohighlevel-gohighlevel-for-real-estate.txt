Is GoHighLevel overkill for a 2 or 3 agent team?
Sometimes, and I'll be straight about when. If your team already gets its leads from one portal, works them by hand and has no plans to run ads, build funnels or reactivate a database, a real estate CRM that comes pre-built will get you going faster. Follow Up Boss is the common pick there: it's built only for real estate, with lead source connections like Zillow ready to switch on, and it does that part really well.
GoHighLevel starts making sense once you want the CRM, texting, calls, booking links, landing pages, email, reviews and ads in one place, and you're willing to build (or pay someone to build) the workflows. Users are unlimited on every plan, so a three agent team pays $97, the same as a solo agent. The cost is setup time, not seats. How the CRM side works in general is in GoHighLevel CRM guide.
One honest warning from my own videos: I'm not a big fan of plug-and-play templates. In my real estate walkthrough I said "I'm not the biggest fan of pre-built templates and snapshots because I feel like it's always best to do a custom tailored solution to the client's needs." A snapshot is a decent starting point, but plan to change it.
Which pipelines should a real estate agent set up?
Keep buyers and sellers in separate pipelines, because the stages and the follow-up are different. Build them under Opportunities > Pipelines. HighLevel's own real estate playbook uses Inquiry, Showing, Offer, Contract and Closed, which is a good skeleton. This is how I'd lay out the ones most people need.
For wholesalers, keep a separate cash buyer list (a tag or smart list is enough) so the moment a property goes under contract you can send it to buyers who asked to hear about deals.

Stages

New lead > Contacted > Pre-approved > Showing booked > Offer made > Under contract > Closed > Past client

New lead > Contacted > Appointment > Offer made > Under contract > Assigned to buyer > Closed > Dead (nurture)

Inquiry > Application > Docs in > Pre-approved > In underwriting > Funded > Annual check-in
Suggested stages. Rename them to match how your team talks.
How do you set up speed to lead for Zillow, portal and Facebook leads?
Every lead source should end in the same workflow: create the contact and an opportunity in the right pipeline, send a text and an email inside the first minute, then get a human on the phone. In my walkthrough I showed this as a call request going to the realtor that's "almost instant" after any ad form is submitted, so they can follow up with their buyer ASAP.
The phone part uses the workflow Call action. HighLevel calls the agent assigned to the contact first (or the company phone if nobody is assigned), plays a whisper like "New Zillow buyer lead, 42 Oak Street", and once the agent answers it dials the lead and bridges the two. Add round robin assignment before it and a team shares the leads evenly.
Facebook and Instagram lead forms connect natively. The full setup and the fixes for leads not coming in are in Facebook lead ads.
Zillow is the tricky one, since HighLevel has no native Zillow integration. Two ways in: Zapier has a Zillow Tech Connect to LeadConnector integration that sends new Zillow leads into the CRM, or you forward the lead notification emails into HighLevel and use the Inbound Email trigger with the AI Email Parser action. HighLevel's own help doc for the parser uses a real estate lead notification as its example. It pulls name, email, phone and message out of the email, but it doesn't save anything by itself, so add Create Contact and Create Opportunity right after it. It's a premium action billed per run. The same email trick works for Realtor.com and most other portals that send lead emails.
What are the best GoHighLevel automations for realtors?
These are the ones I'd build first, in this order. Each one runs inside Automation > Workflows.
Missed call text back. Agents are at showings all day. When a call goes unanswered, a text goes out within a minute or so: "Hey, it's Sarah from the brokerage, sorry I missed your call, what can I help you with?" I built this for a listing agent client who was following up "way too late" with her leads. As I put it in that video, if someone calls and gets no pickup and no text, "you're going to move on to the next realtor and go with whoever picks up first." The build is in Missed call text back.
Buyer and seller nurture. A buyer who isn't ready gets a long, slow sequence of market updates and guides. A seller who asked for a home value gets a valuation drip. My walkthrough has a free home value tag that drops sellers into exactly that sequence. The money is made in the follow-ups, and in real estate those follow-ups can run for a year or more.
Appointment booking and reminders. Booking links for buyer consults, listing appointments and showings, with text and email reminders before each one. If you want AI to book them from text conversations, that's in Build an AI appointment setter.
Review requests after closing. Move the opportunity to Closed and the workflow asks for a review a few days later. Send everyone the same request (no review gating). Setup is in Google review automation.
Past client and database reactivation. This is the super underrated one. In my walkthrough I said "database reactivation is huge especially for realtors", because agents collect lists of 10, 20, 30,000 people over the years. A short campaign with a useful hook (what changed in the market, what their home might be worth now) gets old contacts replying. Add birthday and home purchase anniversary texts to keep past clients in touch, and for mortgage brokers an annual mortgage check-in.
Does GoHighLevel have a dialer for real estate cold calling?
Yes, and it's better than most agents expect. The web dialer is built in, calls log to the contact on their own, and as of November 11, 2026 outbound calls on LC Phone are $0.0166 a minute.
Local presence dialing picks the caller ID closest to the contact's area code, then the same region, then your default number. It works in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia, from the web dialer, the mobile app and desk phones, and repeat calls to the same person use the same number. Turn it on at Settings > Phone System > Voice > Other Settings > Outbound Calls > Default number preference > Local presence dialing. You need a few local numbers in the areas you call for it to do anything.
The multi-line dialer rings one, two or three contacts at once and connects you to whoever picks up first. It's in private beta as of November 11, 2026, so ask support if you don't see the Multi-line dialing switch on that same Outbound Calls screen. For wholesalers and teams doing expired or FSBO calls, that's the feature to watch. Every phone setting is covered in Phone system.
One rule for cold calling: scrub your lists against the Do Not Call registry and your own opt-outs before you dial.
Does GoHighLevel have IDX for a real estate website?
No, not natively. I searched HighLevel's help center and the only IDX mentions are example questions for its AI assistant. On HighLevel's ideas board, the "IDX Integration" request has 289 votes and the status "under review" as of November 11, 2026. It was first posted on May 27, 2020.
So the website is a two-part job. You build the site, landing pages and lead forms in HighLevel, and the MLS search comes from an IDX provider. The common setup is IDX Broker with IDXAddons, which makes HighLevel apps that pass leads, saved searches and featured listings into the CRM. On the ideas thread, a Marketplace app developer posted in October 2026 that their app pulls MLS listings in as property records. Either way, you still need your own IDX agreement with your MLS, and their display rules apply.
If you just want a site for single listings, home value requests and buyer guides, HighLevel's builder is fine without IDX. That's what most of the ad funnels in my walkthrough were: a page about one property or one offer, a form, and a workflow behind it.
Can GoHighLevel track properties with custom objects?
Yes, and this is the feature that makes it feel like a real estate CRM. A custom object is its own record type, so a property gets its own fields (address, type, price, square footage, beds) and links to the contacts and vendors around it.
HighLevel's real estate case study describes exactly this: property records with custom fields, association labels to link a property to the buyers who toured it or the seller who owns it, vendors like inspectors linked to the property, and list view filters to find homes by area, price or type. Admins create them from Object Settings. The Template Library also has a Real Estate custom object template so you don't start from a blank screen.
Where it pays off: match a new buyer's criteria to properties you've already got, see every showing on one listing, and trigger workflows from changes to the property record.
What about TCPA and A2P when texting leads?
Texting US leads from HighLevel needs an approved A2P 10DLC registration, and the messages need consent. A lead who filled out your form with a clear opt-in, or who asked about a home, is very different from a list you bought or skip traced. I don't teach cold text blasts to owner lists. A2P campaigns ask how people opted in, and the TCPA penalties are per message.
As of November 11, 2026 the A2P fees are $22.50 one time for a Sole Proprietor or Low Volume Standard brand, $64 for High Volume, plus a monthly campaign fee ($2 Sole Proprietor, $10 for standard use cases, $1.50 for Low Volume Mixed). The step-by-step is in A2P 10DLC registration.
This isn't legal advice. Mortgage brokers and brokerages in particular should run their texting and consent wording past their compliance person or a lawyer.

How do you sell GoHighLevel to real estate agents?
If you run an agency, agents and brokerages are a great niche because the value is easy to see. Don't pitch "a CRM with workflows". In my realtor video I said "the end result is what you have to sell and what you have to show", not that a text goes out when a call is missed. Show them a lead getting a text in the first minute and their phone ringing with the lead on the other end.
Build the demo before the meeting. I built a real estate voice agent in under 10 minutes on video and fed it a Google Doc with listings and common call scenarios as its knowledge base, which is a great trick when the agent's website is thin. The voice agent build is in Build an AI voice agent.
This is a real business model and I run my agency on it. HighLevel's own award tiers go to agencies with 100+, 500+ and 1,000+ client accounts, so people do this at scale. You could build this for a brokerage and charge a monthly fee you set, with their texting and calls rebilled to them. My process for local businesses is in Sell AI to local businesses.

Is GoHighLevel good for real estate agents?+
Yes, if your business runs on follow-up. It handles speed to lead, texting, calling, nurture, booking and reviews in one place. It has no native IDX, and it takes real setup time.
Does GoHighLevel integrate with Zillow?+
Not natively. Use the Zillow Tech Connect to LeadConnector integration on Zapier, or forward Zillow lead emails into HighLevel and parse them with the AI Email Parser workflow action.
Does GoHighLevel have IDX?+

Does GoHighLevel have a power dialer?+

Can wholesalers use GoHighLevel?+
