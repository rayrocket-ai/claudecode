The four tests for a front-end offer
A lot of people pick their first offer because it's easy to explain. Ads and social media content are easy to explain. Every owner knows what an ad is. But you don't control the result: the algorithm, the creative, the budget and the market all sit between your work and their phone ringing. That's the SMMA problem in one line, and it's why I don't lead with ads.
So before I sell something as the first thing a business buys from me, it has to pass four tests.
1. It's said in words the owner already uses. Calls, customers, reviews, bookings. If you have to explain the word first ("conversational AI", "CRM", "automation"), it's not a front-end offer.
2. You can demo it on their own business in under 5 minutes. Call them after hours, then call the agent you built for them. Show them their own Google listing next to the top three. No slides.
3. They see a result within a week. A missed call that got a text back tonight. Three new reviews by Friday. Not "give it six months".
4. You control the result. If it works or doesn't depends on what you built, not on an ad platform or on whether the owner remembers to do something.
Everything in Level 1 below passes all four. Level 2 passes most of them once you've said one sentence or shown a short demo. Level 3 fails them on purpose, which is why it's the back end.

Where each Level 1 build lives
Never miss a call: the text-back version is in Missed call text back and the receptionist is in Build an AI voice agent. Set the receptionist to after hours or backup if they have a front desk, so nothing changes during the day.
More reviews: Google review automation. Ask every customer the same way. Don't filter who gets the request based on whether they were happy, that's review gating and Google doesn't allow it.
A website that books jobs: AI Studio tutorial. AI Studio came out of Labs for all locations on Sep 22, 2026. Sub-account permissions for it are off by default, so turn them on for the people who need it.
Past customers: AI Campaigns shipped on Oct 27, 2026 and sits behind the Conversations AI Outbound switch in Labs. You run it from Contacts > AI Campaign, or select contacts and click Start AI campaign. The agent writes the first message, follows up, and books when people reply. Only send it to contacts who agreed to hear from the business. The agent setup is in Train Conversation AI.
The newest Level 1 offer: show up on Google Maps and in ChatGPT
This one is new this year, and owners are asking about it before you bring it up. HighLevel now has the pieces to sell it as a measurable fix.
The score. Marketing Audit Reports have an AI Search Visibility section (announced in the LevelUp Day 2026 post on Oct 27, 2026, turned on in Labs). It gives a score out of 100 with four areas: local presence (name, address, phone, listings and reviews across Google and directories), crawler access (whether ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini crawlers are blocked in robots.txt), AI readability (schema, business info, llms.txt, content that needs JavaScript) and agent readiness (can an AI assistant find the phone number, start a call, send an enquiry or book). It ends with a prioritized list of fixes. Open Agency View > Prospecting, or Marketing > Prospecting in a sub-account, add the business as a prospect with the audit on, open the report, pick AI Search Visibility, then Share Report by email, SMS or link.
The heatmap. The same audit has an SEO Heatmap: nine points around the business, the center weighted 20% and each outer point 10%, showing where they rank for a keyword and who the top 3 local competitors are. It needs Premium Prospecting.
The fixes. Listings syncs the business info to 70+ directories (Reputation > Listings in the sub-account). Schema auto-generation (changelog, Jul 2026) adds schema to any website, funnel or webinar page published without it, from Settings > SEO & AEO, and it's on by default for sites created since then. llms.txt (changelog, Jul 2026) is edited per domain in Funnels & Websites > Settings > Domains > Edit Domain and served at yourdomain.com/llms.txt. Add review requests and a clean Google Business Profile and you've covered every area the score checks.
What not to promise. HighLevel's own help doc says the score shows readiness for AI discovery and doesn't promise any AI assistant will recommend the business. So sell the score and the fixes. "You're at this number, here's the list, I'll fix it and we'll rerun it" is something you control. "ChatGPT will recommend you" isn't, and it fails test 4.

Two rules that go with the tables
Rule 1: pick the offer from what you can see. Don't decide you're selling voice agents and then go hunting. Look at the business first: call after hours, read the reviews, check the rank, ask ChatGPT, run the Prospecting audit, open their website on your phone. Whatever is broken in public is your offer. And one genuine test inquiry only. Send one real question through their form or one real call. Never book fake appointments or fill their calendar with test bookings to prove a point.
Rule 2: sell the outcome, the system is the how. "Never miss a call" is the offer. Voice AI, a workflow and a calendar are how you deliver it. The owner doesn't need to hear the second sentence unless they ask. In my video breaking down an agency that sells to mortgage brokers, the point I kept coming back to was: always have some sort of service attached to the software. Don't just hand them the software, because they're going to get confused and they're not going to justify the value on an ongoing basis.
The ladder: front end, core, back end
Every client goes up the same ladder. They start with one Level 1 offer because it's easy to say yes to. Once it works, you add a Level 2 offer that builds on it: the review client gets the past-customer campaign, the missed-call client gets the chat agent and speed to lead. The Level 3 pieces come along with those, and the white-labelled software becomes the monthly product they log into, with your name on it. The white-label setup is in White label guide.
If you want to see what other agencies sell at each level, look at HighLevel's award list. Every agency on it has 100+, 500+ or 1,000+ client sub-accounts (GoHighLevel awards explained), and most of their sites show a simple front-end offer with the GoHighLevel features sold behind it. In my video on this I said to stick to the Gold tier for ideas, because those are easier to copy than the big personal brands at the top.
How to charge for it: setup fee, monthly fee and rebilled usage
You set your own prices. The structure most agencies use, and the one I use, is a one-time setup fee for the build plus a monthly fee to run and maintain it. The monthly part makes sense because the system keeps working every month. Price on the problem you fix and the work involved, not hours.
Some offers come with a product HighLevel lets you resell at your own price. Premium Prospecting costs $29/month per sub-account (gohighlevel.com/pricing, Nov 4, 2026). In Agency View you enable it under Reselling, set the client price, and can give one client a different price. The client clicks Subscribe Now inside Prospecting, pays you, and HighLevel charges your agency its cost. Listings is $30/month per sub-account on the same page. You set the client price with a slider in Agency View > Reselling > Listings, offer monthly, 6-month or annual terms, and the client activates it from Reputation > Listings. You can also turn it on without charging the client and include it in your monthly fee.
Then there's usage: texts, calls, emails and AI. On the $297 Unlimited plan you can rebill phone and email at cost. On the $497 Founder plan you can add your own markup and use SaaS mode (gohighlevel.com/pricing, Nov 4, 2026). For Conversation AI and Voice AI there's a choice per product and per client: a markup on HighLevel's token cost, or a fixed rate per message or per minute that you set. The fixed rate is easier to explain to a small business that doesn't know what a token is. You set it in Manage Client > Rebilling settings, in a SaaS plan, or from the AI usage dashboard. Rates are in Usage fees, and SaaS mode is in SaaS mode guide.

FAQ
What should I sell first with GoHighLevel?+
A Level 1 offer that matches what you can see is broken: never miss a call, more Google reviews, showing up on Google Maps and in ChatGPT, a website that books jobs, reactivating past customers, or fewer no-shows. Each one uses words the owner already uses, can be demoed in minutes and shows a result within a week.
Should I sell GoHighLevel as an all-in-one CRM?+
Not as the first thing. "All-in-one CRM" is a feature list and fails the first test, because owners don't use those words. Lead with an outcome, then make your white-labelled software the monthly product once the front-end offer is working.
Can I sell AI search visibility or ChatGPT rankings with GoHighLevel?+
You can sell the AI Search Visibility score from the Marketing Audit Report and the fixes it lists: listings, reviews, schema, llms.txt, crawler access and a site an AI assistant can book through. HighLevel says the score doesn't promise any AI assistant will recommend the business, so don't sell a ranking in ChatGPT.
Is running ads a good first offer for a GoHighLevel agency?+
Not on its own. Ads are easy to understand but you don't control the result. Sell ads only with the follow-up attached, so every lead gets a reply in under a minute, and only after the business has a proper Google listing, reviews and a website.
How do I know which offer a business needs before I contact them?+
Look for the public signal: an unanswered call after hours, reviews that say nobody called back, a low review count against the top 3, a weak rank or ChatGPT answer, no website, ads running with slow follow-up, or price questions left unanswered on Instagram. HighLevel's Prospecting audit puts several of these in one report.
How should I price GoHighLevel services?+
Most agencies charge a one-time setup fee plus a monthly fee, and you set both. Some products, like Premium Prospecting and Listings, can be resold at a price you set, and usage like AI messages and calls can be rebilled at a markup or a fixed rate you choose.
Can I resell GoHighLevel's Listings and Prospecting tools?+
Yes. Both are set up in Agency View > Reselling, where you choose the client price, and the client subscribes from inside their sub-account. As of Nov 4, 2026, HighLevel lists Premium Prospecting at $29/month and Listings at $30/month per sub-account.
