How to build an AI appointment setter in GoHighLevel (2026, step by step)
By Hamza Baig / Published Oct 31, 2026 / Updated Oct 31, 2026
Heads up: the trial and plan buttons on this page use my affiliate links. If you sign up through one I get a commission. It doesn't cost you more.
To build an AI appointment setter in GoHighLevel, go to AI Agents > Conversation AI, create a Prompt Based Bot from the Appointment Booking template, train it on the business, connect a calendar under Bot Goals, assign the channels (SMS, web chat, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp), test it, then switch it from Suggestive to Auto-Pilot. It replies to every lead in seconds, asks your qualifying questions and books them straight into the calendar.
I have one of these running in my own Instagram DMs right now, basically selling for me 24/7, and I've built them for clients too, like a daycare that needed its tours booked. This guide is the text side. If you want the AI to answer phone calls, see Build an AI voice agent. Below is the full build in the October 2026 version of HighLevel, including the new outbound triggers and the Claude and Gemini models, plus what it costs to run.

How to build it, step by step
01
Connect the channels first
In the client's sub-account, connect everything the bot should answer: an SMS number that's registered for texting (see A2P 10DLC registration), the Facebook page and Instagram account, the chat widget on their site, and WhatsApp if they use it (WhatsApp is a $10 a month add-on per sub-account). The bot can only reply on channels that are connected and assigned to it.
02
Create the bot
Go to AI Agents > Conversation AI > + Create Bot and pick Prompt Based Bot, then Appointment Booking. Guided Form Setup works for a simple bot, but only prompt-based bots can book across multiple calendars or a services calendar, so start there.
03
Write the prompt
Who it is, which business, the tone, what it should qualify for, when to send people to book, what it must never say, and what to do when it doesn't know. Use Additional Instructions for rules like one question at a time and short replies. My starting prompt is below.
04
Train it on the business
Open Bot Training and attach a Knowledge Base (in the newer interface you build it under AI Agents > Knowledge Base). Crawl the website but only tick the pages that answer customer questions, then add FAQs, a table of services and prices, and the policies.
05
Set the bot goals and booking
In Bot Goals, choose the contact details to collect (name, email, phone), enable Appointment Booking and pick the calendar. Then the post-booking options: pause the bot after booking, trigger a workflow, and whether it can cancel and reschedule.
06
Add the actions
Under Set Up Your Actions add Human Handover (for when someone asks for a person or the bot gets stuck) and Auto Follow-up (up to 5 follow-ups when a lead goes quiet, says they're busy, or asks you to check back later).
07
Check the bot settings
Assign the channels, make it the primary bot if it should answer all general inbound messages, add a short response delay so it doesn't reply in half a second, and look at the max messages per conversation (1 to 100, default 100).
08
Test it, then go live
Chat with it in the test panel, then run Prompt Optimizer if you have it in Labs. Put it in Suggestive mode for real leads first, read what it drafts, fix the prompt, and switch to Auto-Pilot when the drafts are ones you'd send yourself.
How I use one in my own Instagram DMs
Every message that comes into my business lands in the Conversations tab: SMS, email, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook. A lot of what you'd see in there are Instagram DMs being answered by AI.
The flow is simple. Somebody comments on one of my videos, a workflow sends them a DM, and the AI agent picks it up from there with a question like: are you looking to start an agency, or are you looking to buy his services? Then it sends them to the right offer depending on the answer. It runs all day and all night, which is the point. The money is made in the follow-ups, and the AI never forgets one.
For clients it's the same idea pointed at bookings. I built an AI appointment setter for a daycare whose problem was following up with leads, qualifying them and getting tours booked. The bot does the qualifying and books the tour, and a nurture sequence keeps working on the ones who aren't ready yet.
The prompt I'd start with
Most of the result comes from the prompt. Keep it plain and specific. Something like this, then swap in the business details:
"You're the booking assistant for [business], a [type of business] in [city]. Sound friendly and professional. Your job is to answer questions about our services and book a [consultation, tour, free checkup] for people who are a good fit. Ask one question at a time. Before booking, find out [criteria 1], [criteria 2] and [criteria 3]. If they fit, offer to book. If they don't, thank them and let them know the team will follow up. Only answer from the information you've been given. If you don't know, say you'll check with the team and get back to them. Never make up prices, availability or medical, legal or financial advice. Keep replies short, like a text from a real person."
Two things from my own builds. Give it the context of the first message the lead got, so a reply of "yes" makes sense to it. And tell it what to do when it's stuck, because "I'll check and get back to you" is always better than a confident wrong answer.
[+]Don't type available times into the prompt. The bot reads real availability from the calendar you connect.
[+]Customer-facing details about each appointment type go in the prompt or Knowledge Base. Calendar descriptions are only used to pick the right calendar.
[+]Use Additional Instructions for style rules: no emojis, mirror the customer's language, one question per message.
Suggestive vs Auto-Pilot: which mode to use
Every bot has three modes. Off does nothing. Suggestive drafts a reply in the message box and a person on the team reviews it, edits it and hits send. Auto-Pilot replies on its own.
New bots can start in Off, so check the mode before you wonder why nothing's happening. Run a new client's bot in Suggestive for the first stretch so the owner sees exactly what it would say, then flip it to Auto-Pilot. HighLevel's own docs say to test booking, FAQs and unknown questions before turning on Auto-Pilot. It also makes the owner feel in control, which helps when you're selling it.
One calendar or several?
Single calendar is the fastest to launch and fine for a business with one appointment type. If they have a few, like a consult and a treatment, pick Multiple Calendars in the Appointment Booking goal, give each calendar a clear description and keywords, and set a fallback calendar for anything that doesn't match. Multiple calendars and services calendars only work on prompt-based bots.
A few limits to know: the bot can't take payment during booking, and private services aren't supported. It does handle "tomorrow" properly even if the lead replies the next morning, and it won't book a contact into a time they already have an appointment.
Handing off to a human
Turn on Human Handover in Bot Goals (it's a Labs feature, so you may need to enable it first). It has three scenarios: the person asks for a human (add phrases like "can I talk to someone real"), the bot doesn't have the information, or it failed to resolve the issue after two tries.
When it hands off it can assign the conversation to a team member, send a final message like "Thanks! Someone from the team will get back to you", pause itself for as long as you set, create a task due in 24 hours, and tag the contact. Make sure the staff member has notifications on for assigned conversations under Settings > My Staff, or the handoff goes nowhere.
New in October 2026: let the AI text new leads first
Before this you needed a workflow to send the first text and then hand the reply to the bot, which is how I built my dental setter back in January 2025. Since Oct 27, 2026 the agent can start the conversation itself. In the agent go to Deploy > Outbound > Triggers and pick from nine: form submitted, Facebook or Instagram lead form, Google lead form, TikTok lead form, contact created, tag added, appointment booked or confirmed, opportunity created or moved, and missed call.
Speed to lead is the whole game here. A sales rep takes hours to get back to a Facebook lead, the AI does it in seconds, when the lead is most interested. Missed call text-back is the other easy win for a local business.
The rules: it's in Labs (Settings > Labs > Conversation AI | Outbound AI Agents), outbound only goes out by SMS, you need a sending number and a compliance confirmation before you can activate it, and it's one trigger of each type per agent. Leads that come in at 2 a.m. get held until working hours start. Do Not Disturb contacts are skipped and the first message carries an AI disclaimer. Only text people who opted in.
Which AI model should it run on?
Since Oct 27, 2026 you're not stuck on OpenAI. Turn on Conversations AI | Claude & Gemini Model Support in Settings > Labs and the Model menu lists Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Haiku 4.5, and Gemini 3.8 Flash, 3.7 Flash and 3.5 Flash Lite next to the GPT models. Each one shows a relative cost from $ to $$$$.
For a booking bot on SMS, a fast cheap model is usually plenty. Save the top models for longer sales conversations. You can also set a secondary model from a different provider so the bot keeps working if the main one goes down. Test again after you switch, because each model writes differently. And if any of your bots were on GPT 5 or GPT 5 Mini, move them, those retired on Oct 30.
Test it before a real lead ever sees it
Don't just send it three friendly messages and hand it over. On my voice agents I run simulations before anything goes live: generate a bunch of scenarios, including weird ones like someone asking for an appointment yesterday or demanding a human, run them all, read what failed, fix the prompt and run it again until it passes almost everything.
Conversation AI has the same thing now. Prompt Optimizer (in Labs) builds scenarios from your prompt, knowledge base and booking setup, runs real chats against a copy of your bot, scores each one pass or fail, and suggests an improved prompt with a side by side diff. Your live prompt doesn't change until you click Use Prompt.
One catch: the actions still run during tests, so point the bot at a test calendar and test workflows first or you'll fill the client's calendar with fake bookings. Then test from your own phone on every channel, ask it something outside its knowledge, and try to cancel and reschedule.

Text or voice: which should you build first?
Text is the easier first build. It covers web forms, lead ads, Instagram and Facebook DMs and missed-call text-backs, and leads can reply when it suits them. Voice is for businesses whose leads mostly call. A business can have both, booking into the same calendar. The voice build, settings and per-minute costs are in my AI voice agent guide.
Why isn't my Conversation AI bot replying?
Check in this order: the mode isn't Off, the channel is connected and assigned to the bot, the bot is set as primary (non-primary bots only reply inside workflows), the bot hasn't hit its max messages and gone to sleep, and the sub-account hasn't used up its AI allowance with Extra Usage off. A sleeping bot wakes up when you mark the conversation as Read, or with the Update Conversation AI Bot and Status workflow action.

FAQ
What is an AI appointment setter?+
An AI that replies to leads over text, DMs or chat, asks qualifying questions and books them into a calendar without a person doing the back and forth. In GoHighLevel it's built with Conversation AI.
Where is Conversation AI in GoHighLevel?+
In the sub-account, under AI Agents > Conversation AI. That's where you create the bot, train it, set its goals and see the dashboard.
Which channels can the GoHighLevel AI appointment setter answer?+
SMS, email, Facebook Messenger, Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, the chat widget and live chat. Outbound messages from the new triggers go out by SMS only.
Can GoHighLevel's AI book appointments?+
Yes. Enable Appointment Booking in Bot Goals and connect a HighLevel calendar. It offers real available times, books the slot, and can cancel and reschedule if you allow it. It can't collect payment during booking.
Is Conversation AI free in GoHighLevel?+
No. It's pay per use by tokens, or included in the AI Employee Growth plan ($50 a month, 1,000 replies) or Unlimited plan ($97 a month, fair use), as of October 2026. Text messages are billed separately.
Can the AI hand the conversation to a human?+
Yes, with the Human Handover action. It can trigger when someone asks for a person, when the bot doesn't know the answer, or after two failed attempts, then assign the conversation, create a task and pause the bot.
Can I use Claude or Gemini instead of ChatGPT?+
Yes, since Oct 27, 2026. Turn on Claude & Gemini Model Support in Settings > Labs and pick the model in the agent's Model menu. Each agent can run on a different model.
