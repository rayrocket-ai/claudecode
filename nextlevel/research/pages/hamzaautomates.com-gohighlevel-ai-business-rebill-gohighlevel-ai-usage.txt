How to price and rebill GoHighLevel AI usage to clients
By Hamza Baig / Published Nov 12, 2026 / Updated Nov 12, 2026
Heads up: the trial and plan buttons on this page use my affiliate links. If you sign up through one I get a commission. It doesn't cost you more.
To rebill GoHighLevel AI to a client, turn on rebilling for their sub-account (Agency View > Sub-Accounts > Manage Client > Rebilling) and pick a pricing model for Conversation AI and for Voice AI separately: a markup on HighLevel's token cost, or a fixed rate per message or per minute that you set. HighLevel always charges your agency wallet the real cost first, rebilling AI usage needs the $497 Founder plan, and nothing stops a usage spike until you set a limit to Block AI (as of Nov 12, 2026).
This page is the margin side only: which model to pick, where each one can lose you money, how to cap spikes and agent loops, and how to show a client what they used. Every per-minute and per-token rate is on Usage fees, and what each AI Employee plan includes is on GoHighLevel AI tools.

Which HighLevel plan you need to rebill AI
HighLevel's AI docs are clear on this one: agencies must be on the $497 plan (now called Founder) to rebill AI Employee usage, and markup rebilling is $497 only (help docs, Nov 12, 2026). The pricing page lists "Pass-through billing for usage and add-ons" on the $297 Unlimited plan, and its FAQ mentions premium AI usage as rebillable on Unlimited too. Those two don't fully line up, so if you're on $297, open Manage Client > Rebilling on a test sub-account and check whether the AI products are there before you quote anyone.
Phone and email are a separate story: $297 rebills them at cost and $497 adds your markup. I'm on the $497 plan. The full plan comparison is on Pricing, and the SaaS mode side (plans, signups, the configurator) is on SaaS mode guide.

Conversation AI and Voice AI are billed separately now
Until Jul 30, 2026, both products sat under one legacy Conversation AI billing product. HighLevel split them, so each has its own line in billing usage, its own rebilling settings, and its own pricing model. The changelog says it plainly: settings applied to one product won't automatically apply to the other.
So if you set up a client's rebilling before August, open it again. Agency View > AI Suite > Plans & Limits, find the sub-account, three-dot menu > Configure Rebilling, and check both products. A client you thought was on a 2x markup for everything might have Voice AI on whatever the split left it at.
The split is also what makes per-product pricing possible. Chat and calls cost very different amounts, so pricing them the same way rarely makes sense.
How the fixed per-unit price works, and where it loses money
Fixed rate shipped Aug 7, 2026. You pick Fixed Rate instead of Markup for a product, enter a price per unit, and click Save Markups. You can set it in three places: Manage Client > Rebilling for one sub-account, the Rebilling step of a SaaS plan in the SaaS Configurator, or the AI Usage Dashboard. It applies to future charges only. HighLevel's own examples are $0.50 per Conversation AI message and $0.10 per Voice AI minute.
The catch is in HighLevel's FAQ: your agency is still charged the actual token cost, and if your fixed rate is lower than that, "you absorb the difference." Two things push the real cost around while your rate stays put.
Voice AI: the model. As of Nov 10, 2026, speech-to-speech is $0.10 a minute on Gemini 3.8 Live and $0.20 on GPT Realtime 2. Classic voice is $0.045 a minute for the engine plus $0.015 to $0.17 for the voice, plus tokens. An illustration, not a recommendation: if you used HighLevel's example rate of $0.10 a minute and someone later switched the agent to a $0.20 model, every minute would cost you $0.10 more than you bill. On 300 minutes in a month, that's $30 out of your pocket. Phone charges sit on top of all of it on every plan.
Conversation AI: the length of the conversation. It's billed by tokens, and input tokens include the whole conversation history, the instructions and the knowledge base it pulls in. So reply 15 in a long thread costs more than reply 1, while a per-message rate charges them the same. HighLevel's own example conversation (100,000 input and 25,000 output tokens at $1.25 and $10 per million) costs $0.375 in total. Read your real numbers in the Usage Dashboard before you set a rate, and look at your longest conversations, not the average.
If you want fixed rate's simple bill without guessing, lock the model in the agent so nobody upgrades it quietly, and recheck the AI pricing doc when HighLevel adds models. The list changes often.
AI Employee plan or pay per use, per client
The other way to make a client's AI bill predictable is a plan. Growth ($50 a month per sub-account) includes 100 Voice AI minutes and 1,000 Conversation AI responses, and Unlimited ($97) covers both under fair use (AI Product Pricing, Nov 10, 2026). You resell either at your own price in Agency View > Reselling > Subscriptions > AI Employee, then switch it on per client in Manage Client > Reselling.
Do the break-even per client. On a $0.10 a minute voice model, Unlimited pays for itself at about 970 minutes a month. On a $0.20 model it's about 485. Below that, pay per use with rebilling is cheaper for the client. These are the same numbers as Usage fees, worked from the same rates.
Three things plans don't cover: phone charges (always separate), the AI Agent workflow action and Flow Agents (pay per use on every plan). Managed Agents get 100 runs on Growth and 1,000 on Unlimited. If a client's build leans on the AI Agent action, a plan won't make their bill flat. More on that in AI Agent action.
When a Growth allowance runs out, the AI pauses by default. Extra Usage in AI Suite keeps it running at pay-per-use rates. For a receptionist answering real calls, decide that before it happens, because a paused agent at 9pm is a missed call.
Usage spikes and agent loops: how to cap them
Every AI bill horror story is the same shape: something ran far more than anyone expected and nothing was set to stop it. An agent replying to an auto-responder, a workflow that re-triggers itself, a tag that fires a Managed Agent on every update, a voice call nobody hung up. Rebilling doesn't fix that. It just moves the bill to your client, which is worse for the relationship.
HighLevel's caps are all off by default, so stack them.

Caps inside the agent itself
Account limits are the backstop. The cheaper fix is building the agent so it can't run away in the first place.
Voice AI: in my receptionist build I point people at call settings, which is where you set "how long the calls can be, how short they can be." I also like selling it as an after-hours agent: "after 8:00 p.m., the agent is going to turn on," so it only works the hours the front desk doesn't. And I keep it on its own line: "there's a human number and then there's a voice receptionist number." All three keep minutes tied to calls the business actually wants answered.
Conversation AI: set max messages so one contact can't trigger 100 replies, and on a booking bot, use the option in the booking action where "you can turn the bot off after it makes a booking." Once the job is done, it stops talking. Setup for both is in Train Conversation AI.
Managed Agents and the AI Agent action: every trigger event is a run, so filter the trigger tightly (one form, one tag, one pipeline stage) and test on 10 contacts before a full list. HighLevel's docs list 30 free test runs a month for Managed Agents. The build side is in Agent Studio.
Block or bill the overage?
This is the actual pricing decision, and it's different per agent.
Block when you're on a flat fee or a fixed plan price, because every unit past the line comes out of your margin. Also block on anything outbound or workflow-driven, where a loop or a bad list can burn through money in an hour.
Bill (keep running, or set SaaS Usage Limits to bill the extra usage) when the AI answers inbound leads and a pause costs the client a customer. A receptionist that goes silent mid-month because of a limit does more damage than the overage. Put that in the agreement up front: what the limit is, what happens at it, and what the extra costs.
Either way, the client should hear about it before the limit, not after. With AI Usage Limits, a client who can't manage their own billing sees a "Request more usage" button when they're blocked, and you approve, upgrade or deny it in AI Suite > Plans and Limits > Notifications > Limit Requests. HighLevel's docs say a sub-account is never billed past its limit. Usage figures can lag up to 5 minutes, so leave a little room.
How to report AI usage to clients
The AI Usage Dashboard (Agency View > AI Suite > Usage Dashboard) is agency-only. A client can't open it from their sub-account. So reporting is on you: pick their sub-account, set the range to Month, and you get total transactions, the amount charged to the wallet, tokens, a usage chart and every transaction. Voice AI transactions show call duration, and sometimes zero tokens, because voice bills by the minute.
Their invoice won't explain it either. With rebilling on, the line shows the AI product name and the total for the period, not whether it's a fixed rate or a markup. For a fixed-rate client that's fine, it's units times your price. For a markup client, send a monthly note with minutes, conversations and the total so the number never surprises them.
SaaS Usage Limits fixes most of this for SaaS clients. Their billing page shows usage against their limit, with color cues as they get close and the date it resets. It's Labs beta for Voice AI and Conversation AI only as of Oct 30, 2026, and HighLevel says phone, email and automations are coming, plus alerts at 50%, 80% and 100%.
Rebilling gotchas before you turn it on
No card, no rebilling. If the sub-account doesn't have a payment method when you attach rebilling, it sits On Hold and the client can have restricted access until they pay. Get the card on file first, or you lock your own client out.
Your wallet still pays first. With rebilling on, HighLevel charges the agency wallet, then your Stripe charges the client's card to refill their wallet. A declined client card doesn't stop HighLevel billing you.
Changes aren't retroactive. Switching a client from markup to fixed rate applies to future usage only.
Agency users get Ask AI and AI Studio free under fair use, but a client's own team using them is pay per use unless they have an AI Employee plan. If you give clients logins, that usage shows up too.

FAQ
Can you rebill Conversation AI and Voice AI in GoHighLevel?+
Yes. Turn on rebilling for the sub-account and set each product on its own, since they've been separate billing products since Jul 30, 2026. HighLevel's AI docs say rebilling AI usage needs the $497 Founder plan (as of Nov 12, 2026).
What's the difference between markup and fixed-rate rebilling?+
Markup charges the client HighLevel's token cost times your multiplier, so their bill moves with usage. Fixed rate charges a flat price per Conversation AI message or Voice AI minute that you set. HighLevel still bills you the real cost, so with a fixed rate you absorb any gap.
Can I lose money rebilling AI at a fixed rate?+
Yes. If the token cost of a message or minute goes above your fixed rate, HighLevel's docs say you absorb the difference. Longer conversations and pricier voice models are the usual causes, so check real costs in the AI Usage Dashboard before setting the rate.
How do I stop a client's AI usage from spiking?+
Set an AI Usage Limit on the sub-account and change it to Block AI at the limit, since the default only notifies. Then cap the agent: call duration on Voice AI, max messages on Conversation AI, and tight triggers on Managed Agents.
Can clients see their own AI usage?+
Not in the AI Usage Dashboard, which is agency-only. SaaS clients with SaaS Usage Limits (Labs beta since Oct 30, 2026) see usage against their limit on their billing page. Otherwise you pull the numbers and send them.
Is an AI Employee plan cheaper than rebilling pay per use?+
Only at volume. Unlimited is $97 a month per sub-account and breaks even around 970 voice minutes on a $0.10 a minute model (rates as of Nov 10, 2026). Phone charges and the AI Agent workflow action are never covered by a plan.
What happens when a client hits their AI limit?+
With Block AI at the limit, the AI pauses until you raise the limit or the month resets. A client who can't manage their own billing can click Request more usage, and you approve, upgrade or deny it in AI Suite.
