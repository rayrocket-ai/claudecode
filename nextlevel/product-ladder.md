## Rung 2. A low-cost self-serve entry

**The question.** The round 2 persona test showed very small practices want something under $100 a month. What can honestly be offered at that price without Ray's time?

**Recommendation: do not sell self-serve software under $100 a month now. Sell a kit instead, and revisit software later behind a gate.**

**Why not software at that price today.**
1. **The costly parts do not shrink.** Each account still needs a phone number, carrier registration for texting, a tested voice prompt and usage billing. Those are the steps that take human time and cause support tickets.
2. **Support has no owner yet.** At under $100 a month, one support call uses up the month's margin. The margin at $300 with usage at cost is itself still an open question in `README.md`.
3. **It would undercut the founders.** A founder pays $300 for Answer. A cheaper plan with any voice AI in it breaks the promise that founders have the best price.
4. **The people who asked for it are not the current buyer.** In the test they were bookkeepers, salons and tax preparers. Their industries are behind the gate and on the waitlist.
5. **Unchecked messages would leave from Ray's platform.** Self-serve users writing their own texts on Ray's agency create consent and carrier exposure with nobody checking the messages.
6. **The under-$100 software licence market is the crowded part** of the researched model. It competes on price alone.

**What can honestly be offered instead.**

**Name (proposed).** **Next Level Field Kit.** PROPOSED, Ray to approve.

**Buyer.** A solo agent or a very small practice that already has a CRM or phone system and wants to set things up alone.

**Promise in one line.** The scripts, message templates, checklists and setup videos Ray uses, to install in the tools you already have.

**What is delivered.** A download and a short video library. No software, no account, no support line, no AI usage.
- The call-answering script and the questions to qualify a caller.
- Missed-call text-back wording, with the consent and identification lines already in.
- The review request sequence and timing.
- A consent and record-keeping checklist for email and text.
- Five short recorded walkthroughs (screen recordings from Ray Homes with all personal data hidden).
- Updates when Ray changes his own versions.

**By whom.** Team 1 drafts from what already runs on Ray Homes. Team 2 checks and designs. Team 4 delivers by email after payment. Team 6 builds the product page later. Ray: approves the kit once, records the five walkthroughs in one session. No ongoing time.

**Price.** PROPOSED: $79 one time. Reasoning: it must be clearly under one month of the cheapest done-for-you product (Reviews at $150), it must be payable without a call, and a one-time price creates no monthly support expectation. Alternative: [Ray to set]. A buyer who moves to a done-for-you product within 60 days has the kit price credited against setup. PROPOSED.

**The gate.** Stripe is verified. The registered business name is on the receipt. Every template has passed the compliance check. The kit states in plain words that it is not legal advice and that the buyer is responsible for consent in their own messages.

**How a buyer moves up.** The kit shows, honestly, how much setup work there is. Each walkthrough ends with one line: "If you would rather have this set up and run for you, that is Next Level Answer." The kit credit is the bridge.

**Later option, not for now.** **Next Level Starter** (PROPOSED name): self-serve software with text-back, one booking page and review requests, and **no voice AI**. PROPOSED price band: $79 to $97 a month plus usage. It opens only when all of these are true: 10 clients have been onboarded by someone other than Ray; SaaS mode and rebilling are confirmed on Ray's HighLevel plan [verify in HighLevel]; a support process exists with a named owner; message templates are locked so a user cannot send non-compliant texts. Decision 1 below.

## Rung 4. A paid diagnostic for teams and brokerages

**Name (proposed).** **Next Level Lead Path Review.** PROPOSED, Ray to approve. (It is separate from the free missed-call audit in `sales/missed-call-audit.md`, which is a short outreach tactic for single agents.)

**Buyer.** A team leader or a brokerage owner with 5 or more agents who wants to know what happens to every inquiry before buying anything.

**Promise in one line.** A written report that shows where each inquiry to your team goes, how fast it is answered, and what to fix first.

**The deliverable.** A written report of about 10 pages, delivered as a PDF and walked through on one call. The client owns it and may use it with any vendor. PROPOSED contents:
1. **The map.** Every way an inquiry reaches the team (phone lines, website forms, portal leads, social messages, sign calls, open house lists) and where each one lands.
2. **The measurements.** Time to first reply by channel and by time of day. Share of calls answered live. What happens after hours. Each number is taken from the team's own records or from test inquiries, and the report says which.
3. **The follow-up record.** How many touches a new inquiry gets in its first 14 days, from the team's own CRM.
4. **Consent and records check.** Whether opt-in, identification and unsubscribe are present in the team's current emails and texts. Observations only, with a note to take them to counsel.
5. **The gaps, ranked.** Each with the evidence behind it.
6. **The fix list.** For each gap: what to change, whether a Next Level product covers it, and where it does not. Some fixes will be free process changes.
7. **A quote** for the Next Level products that fit, at the team price.

What the report does **not** contain: any estimate of lost income, deals or commission. It reports what was measured.

**The method.**
1. Intake form (15 minutes for the client).
2. A 60-minute interview with the team leader.
3. Read-only access to the phone log and CRM reports for the last 30 days, under a signed confidentiality and data-handling agreement.
4. Up to 15 test inquiries across the team's channels, at times agreed in writing in advance with the team leader, each one identified afterward so no agent chases a false lead.
5. Draft, compliance check, Ray's review.
6. A 45-minute report call.

**By whom.** Team 5 books it and sends the intake. Team 7 turns the exports into the measurements. Team 1 drafts the report from a fixed template. Team 2 runs the compliance check and the layout. Ray: the interview, the test inquiries or their approval, the judgment on what to fix first, the final read, the report call.

**Time it takes.** PROPOSED: 10 business days from the day access is granted. Ray's own time is estimated at 4 to 6 hours for each review; that is an estimate to be measured on the first two, not a known figure.

**Price.** PROPOSED range: $1,500 to $3,500, fixed and quoted before work starts, set by the number of agents and channels. Reasoning: it should sit at or above the setup fee for Complete ($1,500) because it is several hours of Ray's own time; it should stay well under the $5,000 seen in the research because Ray has no team-level client results yet and his buyer is a real estate team, not a mid-size company. PROPOSED: the fee is credited against Next Level setup fees if the team proceeds within 30 days. PROPOSED: the first two reviews are done at $750 each in exchange for feedback and permission to describe the method (not the client's numbers). Final figures: [Ray to set].

**The gate.** At least three founding clients are live, so the fix list rests on a product that is running for others. The data-handling agreement is written with counsel. The report template exists and has been run once on Ray Homes itself.

**How a buyer moves up.** From rung 3: a client who runs a team is offered the review. From the review: the fix list leads to done-for-you products for each agent on the team, or to a Team Licence (rung 5) once that is open.

## What not to copy

From the researched model, these are left out on purpose.

1. **Income, revenue and client-count claims as proof.** Headlines with a monthly revenue figure, counts of agencies served, member totals. Next Level states only the six track-record facts in `README.md` and shows the live system.
2. **Selling the dream of an agency to beginners.** A free course whose purpose is to make people want a platform, then selling them the platform. Next Level's licence is for teams, brokerages and partners who already have the people to serve.
3. **A suggested resale price or a "what you could charge" figure** on the licence page. That is an earnings representation in another form.
4. **Dollar figures in video hooks** attached to a build. Ray's hooks use the stake the agent already feels.
5. **Scarcity that cannot be shown to be true**, such as a spot count next to a price. The Founding 10 is a real limit. Nothing else gets a countdown.
6. **Rising-price pressure.** Founders have a locked price because Ray promised it, not as an urgency device for later buyers.
7. **Teaching the sale before the delivery.** The Operator Course teaches delivery only.
8. **Many industries at once.** One industry at a time, each opened after counsel has confirmed what the AI may say, record and send there.
9. **A low flat licence fee with unlimited accounts and community-only support.** It moves the compliance risk to people least able to carry it, while the messages still leave from Ray's platform.
10. **Old pages, conflicting prices and several subdomains left live.** One price list, in `offer.md`, and every page reads from it.
11. **Paid press releases and contributor columns as proof.** For a registrant, advertising has to be accurate and supportable.
12. **Another company's names, frameworks or wording.** Every name on this page is new and still needs the trademark check.
13. **Any tie between the software and joining Ray's team or eXp.** A recruiting conversation is never a condition, a discount or a step on this ladder.
