# Next Level content: the inbound engine

Draft for Ray to approve. Nothing here posts until Ray approves each piece in Slack #agent-marketing.

The goal Ray set: inbound. Agents and business owners reach out to Ray because of what they saw. No cold outreach is needed for this engine to work.

Everything here is perspective from a working broker. It is not legal or financial advice.

## 1. The one promise

**Every call answered, qualified and booked. Live in 7 days.**

Every piece of content proves one part of that sentence, shown on a real phone line by a broker who runs it on his own business.

What content never does: promise leads, deals, commission or income. It shows the system working and lets the viewer judge.

## 2. The audience

### Lane 1: agents (now)

Working real estate agents and small teams in Canada and the US who take their own calls and miss some during showings, listing appointments and evenings.

What they should think after a video: "That is my phone. He is one of us. I can hear it for myself."

Where it leads: the quiz, then a 15-minute call, then one of the Founding 10 places for Next Level Answer.

### Lane 2: business owners (waitlist only)

Mortgage brokers, real estate lawyers, accountants, contractors and insurance brokers. Same missed-call problem, different day.

The gate in `offer.md` still holds. For this lane, content may show the problem and the system, and invite the owner to join the waitlist by their own choice. Content may not state a price for a non-agent business, offer a sale, or link to a checkout. Industries open one at a time, mortgage brokers first.

Share of the calendar: about 1 piece in 5. Enough to build the waitlist without blurring who the offer is for today.

## 4. The formats

| Format | What it is | Best for pillar | Length |
|---|---|---|---|

| Before and after | A missed-call log next to the same hours with the receptionist on. Ray's own line. | 1, 3 | 45 to 60 seconds |
| Street or office interview | Ray asks one agent or owner one question: "What happens to your phone when you are with a client?" Filmed with their written permission. | 1, 5 | 45 to 75 seconds |
| Long-form YouTube | One 10 to 14 minute lesson a week, filmed in one sitting, cut into 6 shorts. | 2, 3, 4 | 10 to 14 minutes |
| Carousel | 7 slides. One idea per slide. | 1, 4 | 7 slides |

### On-screen rules for every format

- No real caller's name or phone number on screen. Ever. Use Ray's own test calls and blur or crop everything else.
- Any call log or dashboard shown is Ray's own line with caller details hidden.
- The AI says it is an AI assistant at the start of the call. Leave that in the video. It is a strength.
- A real number from Ray's system is shown only when Ray has supplied it. Until then the line reads [Ray to add: real number from Ray Homes] and the piece is filmed as a live demonstration that works without it.
- Interviews need written permission from the person on camera. First name and last initial at most.
- Real human video comes first. Ray is on camera. AI visuals support, they do not replace him.

## 5. The call-to-action ladder

One call to action per piece. The ladder is the same every time.

1. **Comment the keyword.** The video names one word. The viewer comments it.
2. **DM with the resource.** A person sends the resource by direct message. Replies are drafted by an AI and sent by Ray or Diana. Scripts are in `dm-replies.md`.
3. **Quiz page.** Six questions that find the viewer's biggest leak. Copy is in `sales/funnel-copy.md`. Link: [Ray to confirm: quiz page link]
4. **15-minute call.** Ray dials the receptionist live. Link: [Ray to confirm: 15-minute call booking link]

Rules:

- The video asks only for the comment. The DM asks only for the next step. Never stack two asks in one message.
- A comment is permission to reply to that comment by DM about that resource. It is not consent to add anyone to an email or text list. Email and text consent is collected on the quiz page with the unticked boxes already written there.
- If the viewer is not an agent, the DM offers the waitlist and nothing else.

## 8. Posting rhythm: one filming session a day

Ray films once a day, about 20 to 30 minutes.

| Each day | What Ray films | What posts |
|---|---|---|
| Monday to Sunday | 2 shorts from the script file. One in the morning light, one wherever the day takes him. | 2 shorts a day to Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and YouTube Shorts. |

| Each week | What happens |
|---|---|
| One day (Tuesday proposed) | The filming session is the long-form YouTube video instead of 2 shorts, or in addition if time allows. It posts Thursday. Six moments are marked for cutting into shorts. |
| Monday, Wednesday, Friday | One carousel posts to Instagram and Facebook. These are written, not filmed. |
| Monday to Friday | One LinkedIn post, built from that day's stronger short. |
| Saturday | Ray replies to comments in two agent Facebook groups for 15 minutes. |

Weekly output: 14 shorts, 1 long video, 3 carousels, 5 LinkedIn posts. If a day is missed, the next day's scripts move up. Nothing is doubled to catch up.

A buffer: film 2 extra shorts every Sunday so a busy closing week does not break the rhythm.

## 9. Approval: every piece goes through Slack #agent-marketing

Nothing posts without Ray's check mark.

1. The Approval Desk routine runs Monday, Wednesday and Friday morning. On each run it drafts at most one Next Level piece from `calendar-30-days.md` (the next unused day), using the matching script or hook.
2. It saves a MOS Posts row and posts one message in #agent-marketing: "APPROVAL NEEDED", with the full caption exactly as it would go out.
3. Ray reacts with a check mark to approve, an x to reject, or replies in the thread with changes.
4. A piece with a placeholder still in it goes to Ray marked "NOT PUBLISHABLE YET" so he can supply the missing item in the thread.
5. Any price in a piece is listed under "NEEDS RAY'S OK ON FIGURES". Only prices in `offer.md` may appear.
6. Dari longer than two sentences carries the line "Ray to review Dari".
7. Ray's own listing content comes first. When the run reaches its cap, the Next Level piece waits for the next run.

Because the desk drafts at most one Next Level piece per run, Ray approves scripts ahead of filming in batches when he wants to move faster: reply "approve week 1 scripts" in the thread and the desk marks them. [Ray to confirm: batch approval of a whole script week in one thread is acceptable]

DM replies follow the same principle. An AI drafts, Ray or Diana sends.
