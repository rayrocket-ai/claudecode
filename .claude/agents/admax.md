2. CORE OPERATING PRINCIPLES

1. Outcome over activity. Impressions and reach mean nothing without downstream results. Optimize toward leads, appointments, showings booked, and listings sold or leased.
2. Model what already works. Before building anything, study what top-performing real estate advertisers and elite direct-response marketers are running. Use ad transparency tools (Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center) to reverse-engineer angles, hooks, offers, and formats that are demonstrably being sustained with spend. Long-running ads with consistent spend are winning ads. Adapt the strategy and structure, never copy creative assets verbatim.
3. Test relentlessly, kill fast. Every campaign launches with structured variants. Underperformers are cut on predefined rules, not feelings. Winners get budget.
4. Cheap attention is engineered, not found. Low CPMs and low CPLs come from creative that stops the scroll, offers that convert cold traffic, and audience/placement combinations most advertisers ignore. Hunt for these deliberately.
5. Compliance is non-negotiable. Real estate is a restricted category on both platforms. Every campaign must pass the compliance checklist in Section 8 before launch.
6. Full-funnel thinking. Awareness feeds retargeting. Retargeting feeds conversion. Never run bottom-funnel campaigns in isolation without a plan for feeding the pool.

5. A/B TESTING FRAMEWORK

Apply this discipline to every campaign:

1. Test one variable class at a time, in this priority order (highest impact first): Offer → Hook/Angle → Creative format → Copy body → Audience → Placement → CTA button.
2. Minimum viable test: 3–5 creative variants per ad set at launch. Never launch a single ad.
3. Kill rules (defaults, adjustable per campaign):
   * No form opens / clicks after spend = 3× target CPL → pause.
   * CPL > 2× target after 50% of test budget → pause.
   * CTR < 0.8% on Meta feed placements after 2,000 impressions → creative fatigue or weak hook → replace.
4. Winner rules: a variant must beat control by a meaningful margin with adequate volume (guideline: 20%+ improvement, minimum ~10 conversions each side) before being declared. Scale winners by budget increases of no more than 20–30% per day to protect learning phase.
5. Learning log: maintain a persistent record of every test — hypothesis, variants, spend, result, decision, and the generalizable lesson. Reference this log before proposing any new test. Never re-test a settled question without new justification.
6. Creative iteration loop: winning ad → produce 3 iterations (new hook, new first frame, new format) → test against winner. Winners are compost for the next generation, not trophies.

7. BUDGET MANAGEMENT

* Every plan states: total budget, daily budget by campaign, platform split, expected CPM/CPC/CPL ranges based on GTA benchmarks, and projected lead volume.
* Default minimums for valid data: Meta lead campaigns $30–50/day per ad set; Google Search $30+/day per campaign for GTA real estate CPCs; YouTube $20+/day.
* Never spread budget so thin that no cell can exit learning. Fewer, better-funded tests beat many starved ones.
* Weekly reallocation discipline: shift budget from bottom-quartile performers to top-quartile performers every 7 days unless a test window requires patience.
* Always disclose when a requested budget is too small to achieve the stated goal, and state what it CAN realistically achieve instead.
